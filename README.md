# Wittgenstein and Large Language Models: From Logical Representation to Language Practice

**维特根斯坦 × 大语言模型：从逻辑表征到语言实践**

A cross-disciplinary research report on language philosophy, large language models, practice-centered AI architecture, and testable engineering hypotheses.

[Read the full report](./index.html) · [中文版项目说明](#项目简介) · [反馈与讨论](../../issues)

## Project Overview

This project explores a question at the intersection of philosophy of language and AI engineering:

**Can organizing roles, contexts, goals, norms, discourse, actions, consequences, social feedback, and state updates as a unified practice-centered modeling object produce measurable capability gains beyond the strongest matched modular baseline?**

The report uses Ludwig Wittgenstein’s early and later philosophy as an analytical perspective. It does not claim that Wittgenstein anticipated modern AI, nor does it treat philosophical arguments as ready-made computational mechanisms.

It proposes a computational abstraction called a *Practice Episode* and outlines a controlled experimental framework for evaluating its potential engineering value.

## What the Report Covers

* Early and later Wittgenstein: logical representation, language games, rule-following, and forms of life.
* Connections and distinctions between Wittgenstein’s philosophy and contemporary LLMs.
* Pretraining, reinforcement learning, retrieval-augmented generation, memory, tool use, agents, world models, and embodied AI.
* Practice-centered modeling, training objectives, architectures, and evaluation.
* Experimental design, baselines, falsification criteria, alternative explanations, and future research directions.

## Core Research Question

The central empirical question is whether a unified practice-centered organization provides an independent engineering advantage over a strong, jointly optimized modular baseline under matched compute, supervision, and interaction budgets.

The report distinguishes this hypothesis from claims that current models have already demonstrated such an advantage.

## Evidence Status

The hypotheses and experiments described in this report are proposals, not established empirical findings. The core comparison and its controls are intended to make the central claim testable and falsifiable.

The report should be read as a research proposal and analytical framework, not as evidence that the proposed architecture has already outperformed existing systems.

## How to Read

Start with the abstract and the first four chapters for the philosophical motivation.

Readers interested in current AI systems can continue with the chapters on the LLM technology stack. Readers interested in implementation and empirical evaluation can focus on the practice-centered architecture, experimental design, benchmark, and appendices.

## Scope and Limitations

This report connects philosophical analysis with a proposed engineering research program. Philosophical interpretation, structural analogy, interpretive hypothesis, and engineering hypothesis are treated as distinct categories.

The report does not claim that philosophical similarity establishes computational equivalence, or that the proposed framework has been empirically validated.
